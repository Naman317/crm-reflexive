AbstractâThe dominant sequence transduction models in memory architectures are based on complex recurrent or retrieval networks that append historical conversational notes into prompt context. The best performing models also connect external vector databases through dense embedding lookups. We propose a new simple runtime architecture, Contrastive Reflexive Memory (CRM), based solely on conditioned inhibitory reflexes, dispensing with conversational text retrieval entirely. Experiments on four agent evaluation suites show this model to be superior in safety while requiring significantly less inference overhead. Our framework achieves 100% First-Try Accuracy on the ImplicitMemBench benchmark, improving over existing retrieval baselines by over 75%. Across multi-turn evaluations, our framework establishes a high retention rate while operating in 0.15 milliseconds on a commodity CPU. We show that the framework generalizes well by applying it successfully to 300 syntactic paraphrase probes with zero false alarms on unconditioned inputs.
